# Playbook Core Principles

## Purpose

Define enduring principles that guide trade-offs across the Product Development Playbook without replacing its lifecycle rules or detailed standards.

## Scope

These principles apply to decisions made while adopting or using the Playbook. They do not prescribe a product architecture, delivery method, tool, provider, or implementation.

## Principles

### Evidence before assertion

Make decisions from the best available evidence, state meaningful uncertainty, and record assumptions that materially affect a decision. Absence of evidence is a risk to manage, not confirmation.

### Accountable judgment

Automation and AI may assist or perform authorized work, but an accountable owner remains responsible for material decisions, exceptions, and declarations of completion.

### Proportionate rigor

Apply documentation, validation, review, and control in proportion to risk, reversibility, impact, and uncertainty. Avoid both careless omission and process that adds no decision value.

### Small, reversible progress

Prefer bounded increments that can be reviewed, validated, learned from, and adjusted. A stable direction does not require irreversible plans.

### Explicit boundaries

State what is in scope, out of scope, assumed, deferred, or accepted as risk. Do not let convenience, a tool default, or an inferred request silently expand authority.

### Portable practice

Keep governing rules, evidence, and decisions independent of a specific provider or platform. Provider-specific configuration may support work but must not become the only path to continue it.

### Sustainable stewardship

Consider the product's users, operators, maintainers, data, and future change. Delivery is complete only with the evidence and follow-up needed to sustain the outcome.

## Applying Principles

When principles conflict, the accountable owner records the relevant trade-off, evidence, impact, and reassessment condition. Detailed standards own the resulting process and evidence.
